Turn the PDF into an editable Figma file.

A free, ready-made Figma plugin called "PDF to Figma" does this for you. Two clicks to install, one click to run, and the whole document becomes editable Figma content — text you can retype, shapes you can recolor, everything.

What's happening here, in plain English

A "plugin" in Figma is just a little tool other people have made and shared for free. The one we're using takes a PDF and rebuilds it inside Figma as proper editable layers — text becomes typeable text, shapes become real shapes, and so on.

You don't install anything on your Mac. The plugin lives inside Figma. You add it to your account once, and it's there forever.

💡
Honest expectations

These free converters work well — usually 80–90% of things come in clean and editable. A few things sometimes need tidying (a title might be slightly off-position, a font might fall back). It's normal — just nudge things into place after. If too much is broken, scroll to the "Plan B" section at the bottom.

Before you start

  • A free Figma account (works on the website or desktop app — either is fine)
  • About 5 minutes
  • The PDF (you already have it — path is in Step 3 below)

The 5 steps

  1. Open Figma and start a new file

    Go to figma.com in your browser or open the Figma desktop app — both work this time. On the home screen, click "New design file". A blank canvas opens.

  2. Open the plugin search bar

    Look at the top-middle of the Figma window. You'll see a search bar that says something like "Actions" or has a small magnifying glass icon. Click on it (or press / on your keyboard — that's the forward slash key).

    Type these exact words into the search:

    PDF to Figma

    Alternate path if you can't find the search bar: click the Figma icon in the very top-left → Plugins → Manage plugins…. A window opens where you can search the same way.

  3. Pick the plugin and click "Run"

    You'll see one or two results. Pick the one named simply "PDF to Figma" (by Sasha Larionov is the most popular — it'll have a yellow PDF icon). Click on it.

    If Figma asks you to "Install" first, click that — then click "Run". A small panel pops up on your canvas.

    If you don't see that exact one: any plugin called something like "PDF to Figma," "Import PDF," or "PDF Import" will work similarly. Pick whichever has the most users (Figma shows a number).

  4. Upload the PDF into the plugin

    The plugin panel will have a button like "Upload PDF", "Choose file", or a drag-and-drop area. Click the button.

    A file picker opens. The PDF lives here:

    Location on your Mac

    compass-ui-ux-designs/retina/
    └── store-manager-design-system.pdf
    Quick way to get there in the file picker: press Cmd + Shift + G on your keyboard. A little box appears — paste in the folder path above (the part with the slashes), hit Enter, and you'll jump straight to the folder. Double-click the PDF.

  5. Wait, then watch it appear

    The plugin processes the PDF — could take 30 seconds to a few minutes depending on length. You'll see a progress bar or spinner. Don't close the panel while it's working.

    When it finishes, the editable Figma version of every page appears on your canvas. Press Shift + 1 on your keyboard to zoom out and see everything.

Now edit it like any Figma file

  1. Rename the file

    Click "Untitled" at the top of Figma and rename it to "Store Manager — Design System" so it's easy to find later.

  2. Double-click anything to edit it

    Double-click text to retype. Click any shape and change its color from the panel on the right side. Drag things around freely. If something comes in slightly off-position, just select it and move it.

  3. Share when ready

    Click the blue "Share" button in the top-right of Figma to invite someone by email or copy a link. Figma saves automatically.

Something not working?

I can't find the search bar at the top of Figma

Different Figma versions have it in slightly different spots. Try pressing / (the forward slash key) on your keyboard — that always opens it. Or click the Figma icon in the top-left corner → Plugins → Manage plugins… and search from there.

The plugin says I need to install it first

That's normal — just click the "Install" button. It's free, no account upgrade needed. After install, click "Run" on the same screen.

The plugin panel is blank or won't load

Close the plugin (click the X), refresh Figma (Cmd + R), and try again. If you're on the desktop app and it keeps failing, try the same steps in the browser version at figma.com — sometimes one works when the other doesn't.

The fonts look wrong after import

The PDF uses Inter and JetBrains Mono. If you don't have them on your Mac, Figma swaps in a substitute and you'll see a small warning icon. Install both for free from fonts.google.com (search the names), then reopen the file.

Only some pages imported

Free plugins sometimes cap how many pages they convert at once. Try running the plugin a second time on the same PDF — or split the PDF into smaller chunks first. If this is annoying, let me know and we'll go with Plan B above instead.

Everything looks like a giant blurry image

That means the converter fell back to "image mode" instead of doing a real conversion — usually happens with complex PDFs. This is the Plan B trigger: tell me and I'll build the custom helper that produces clean editable output.
